# Business Insights

This document summarizes the main business insights identified from the synthetic retail and wholesale dataset and the analytical outputs generated by the project.

The findings are intended for portfolio demonstration and analytical interpretation rather than operational decision-making.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The dataset contains 85,931 sales records covering 761 sold products and 457 customers.

Total recorded sales amount is approximately 134.02 billion currency units, with several clear concentration patterns across products, categories, customers, and seasons.

The analysis highlights four major themes:

1. Strong concentration of revenue among a small number of customers and products.
2. Lego and construction products represent the largest sales category.
3. Sales show meaningful seasonal variation, with summer representing the strongest season.
4. Association-rule analysis identifies recurring product combinations that may support cross-selling strategies.

The 90-day Prophet forecast estimates approximately 15.86 billion currency units of future sales over the forecast horizon, with daily forecast values ranging from approximately 123.46 million to 216.55 million.

## 3. Customer Concentration

The most significant customer-level finding is the concentration of sales among a small number of customers.

The largest customer, **کلبه سرگرمی بندرعباس**, generated approximately 73.48 billion in sales, representing approximately **54.8% of total recorded sales**.

The next largest customers were substantially smaller:

| Customer | Sales |
|---|---:|
| کلبه سرگرمی بندرعباس | 73.48B |
| تویز کودک مشهد | 5.85B |
| مرکز تویز کیش | 2.91B |
| بچه‌ها سرگرمی ارومیه | 2.09B |
| بچه‌ها اسباب‌بازی کرج | 1.70B |

### Business Interpretation

The dataset demonstrates a significant customer concentration risk.

More than half of recorded sales are associated with a single customer. In a real commercial environment, such concentration could create exposure to customer churn, negotiation pressure, payment risk, or demand volatility.

### Recommended Management Action

Management should monitor customer concentration using metrics such as:

- Revenue share of top customers
- Customer retention
- Revenue-at-risk
- Customer profitability
- Order frequency
- Credit exposure

Customer diversification should be evaluated alongside revenue growth rather than focusing exclusively on total sales.

Sales vary considerably across seasons.

|---|---:|
| Summer | 40.91B |
| Spring | 35.66B |
| Winter | 34.32B |
| Autumn | 23.14B |

Summer represents approximately **30.5% of total recorded sales**, while autumn represents approximately **17.3%**.

### Business Interpretation

The seasonal distribution suggests that demand is not uniform throughout the year.

This creates opportunities for:

- Seasonal inventory planning
- Pre-season replenishment
- Promotional campaigns
- Supplier capacity planning
- Working-capital optimization

Inventory and procurement decisions should therefore consider expected seasonal demand rather than relying solely on historical average demand.

## 7. Inventory & Supply Chain

The inventory dataset contains:

- 312,500 inventory records
- 2,500 tracked products
- Average quantity on hand: 43.72 units
- Total quantity ordered: 2,275,825 units

The inventory layer connects commercial demand with stock availability, replenishment, minimum-stock thresholds, supplier lead times, and order quantities.

### Business Interpretation

The combination of sales and inventory analytics creates an opportunity to prioritize products based on both demand and supply-chain exposure.

Potential management metrics include:

- Stock coverage
- Inventory turnover
- Low-stock exposure
- Reorder priority
- Lead-time risk
- Demand versus available inventory

A particularly useful future enhancement would be to combine product demand forecasts with supplier lead times and minimum-stock thresholds to create a more explicit replenishment recommendation engine.

## 8. 90-Day Sales Forecast

The Prophet forecasting pipeline generates a 90-day forecast.

Forecast summary:

- Forecast horizon: 90 days
- Total forecasted sales: approximately 15.86B
- Average daily forecast: approximately 176.17M
- Minimum daily forecast: approximately 123.46M
- Maximum daily forecast: approximately 216.55M
- Prediction interval: 95%

### Business Interpretation

The forecast provides an analytical estimate of expected sales over the next 90 days and can support planning discussions around inventory, procurement, and commercial activity.

The variation between minimum and maximum predicted daily sales also indicates that demand should not be treated as a constant daily quantity.

### Important Limitation

The forecasting model is presented as an analytical demonstration.

No formal backtesting or custom holiday regressors were applied in this project. Therefore, the forecast should not be interpreted as a validated production demand-planning model.

## 9. Market Basket Analysis

Association-rule mining was performed using the Apriori algorithm.

The analysis uses:

- Minimum support: 0.02
- Minimum confidence: 0.40
- Ranking by lift

The strongest identified rule has:

- Support: 2.27%
- Confidence: 40.21%
- Lift: 1.60

Several other rules show confidence values above 50%, with some exceeding 75%.

### Business Interpretation

A lift greater than 1 indicates that the associated products occur together more frequently than would be expected under an independence assumption.

The identified relationships may therefore support:

- Cross-selling
- Product bundling
- Recommendation systems
- Promotional combinations
- Basket-oriented merchandising

However, association does not imply causality. These rules should be evaluated experimentally before being converted into commercial policies.

## 10. Management Implications

The combined analysis suggests several areas where management attention could create value.

### 1. Reduce Customer Concentration Risk

The extreme concentration of sales in the largest customer represents a potential commercial risk.

Customer diversification and retention should therefore be monitored alongside revenue growth.

### 2. Protect High-Value Product Availability

High-revenue products and categories should receive greater attention in inventory and replenishment planning.

### 3. Incorporate Seasonality into Planning

The strong difference between summer and autumn sales indicates that procurement and inventory decisions should consider seasonal demand patterns.

### 4. Connect Forecasting with Supply Chain Decisions

The forecasting layer becomes more valuable when combined with inventory levels, minimum-stock thresholds, supplier lead times, and order quantities.

### 5. Use Association Rules for Cross-Selling

High-lift product combinations can be candidates for bundles, recommendations, and targeted promotions.

---

## 11. Key Analytical Takeaways

The project demonstrates how a business analytics workflow can connect multiple analytical layers:

**Sales → Customers → Products → Inventory → Forecasting → Association Rules**

The most important findings are:

- Revenue is highly concentrated among a small number of customers.
- Lego and construction products are the largest revenue category.
- The top two categories represent nearly half of total sales.
- Summer is the strongest season in the dataset.
- High-value products can materially influence overall commercial performance.
- The 90-day forecast provides an analytical basis for forward-looking planning.
- Association rules reveal recurring product combinations that may support cross-selling.

---

## 12. Limitations

The dataset is synthetic and therefore does not represent actual company performance.

Additional limitations include:

- No causal inference is performed.
- Forecasting has not been formally backtested.
- Custom holiday effects are not included in the Prophet model.
- Association rules identify relationships rather than causal effects.
- Inventory analysis should be combined with actual operational constraints before making replenishment decisions.
- The 1405 sales period should not be interpreted as a complete-year comparison without confirming the underlying date coverage.

These limitations are intentionally documented to distinguish analytical demonstration from production decision-support systems.
